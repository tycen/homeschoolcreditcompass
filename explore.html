Leads to look into

The list

Grouped loosely; expect this to grow over time. A listing here is an invitation to investigate, not an endorsement.

Sophia Learning & Saylor Academy

Sophia: flat monthly subscription · Saylor: free courses, small exam fee

Two flavors of the same idea: self-paced online courses that earn credit recommended by ACE (the American Council on Education). Sophia is one subscription for as many courses as your student can finish; fast workers bank gen eds very cheaply. Saylor makes the courses themselves free and charges only a small fee for each proctored final, often the lowest cost-per-credit tool on the board.

The fine print

ACE-recommended credit is not the same as accredited coursework; colleges choose whether to honor it, and many traditional campuses don't. Both publish lists of partner schools along with transfer and articulation details, and transfer-friendly online schools (including the degree-by-exam "Big Three" described below) take them readily. Acceptance still depends on the specific course, the destination program, the grade earned, and the current agreement, so check before buying a subscription. If your student's destination honors ACE credit, these are among the cheapest credits available anywhere; if not, skip them. Study.com is another subscription player in this same space.

sophia.org · saylor.org

The "degree by exam" approach

A whole degree, mostly from credit-by-exam

A concept more than a single tool: bank a large block of credit through exams and ACE-recommended courses (CLEP, DSST, Sophia, Saylor), then transfer it into a school built to accept big transfer blocks. The classic "Big Three" here are Excelsior University, Thomas Edison State University, and Charter Oak State College, which grant regionally accredited bachelor's degrees assembled largely from transferred credit.

How it works, and our honest caveat

Each of the Big Three requires a modest residency (a handful of their own courses, often including a capstone), then awards the degree. Done carefully, a student can finish a regionally accredited bachelor's for a small fraction of a traditional degree's price. We haven't walked this path ourselves, so we're listing it as a concept to research rather than a route we can vouch for firsthand. If lowest-total-cost is the goal and your student is highly independent, it's worth a serious look.

excelsior.edu · tesu.edu · charteroak.edu

DSST Exams

Cost: similar to CLEP · free for military via DANTES

CLEP's lesser-known sibling: credit-by-exam tests originally built for the military but open to everyone. Around 30 subjects, and the catalog covers ground CLEP doesn't: ethics, criminal justice, personal finance, technical writing, astronomy, and more. Roughly 1,500 colleges accept them.

When DSST beats CLEP

Use DSST when the subject your student has already mastered simply isn't in CLEP's catalog, or when a college's equivalency table happens to be more generous on the DSST side. Fewer colleges accept DSST than CLEP, so the registrar check matters even more here. Military families: DANTES funds the exam fee for service members.

getcollegecredit.com

mikeroweWORKS Foundation

Scholarships for the skilled trades

Mike Rowe's foundation champions the skilled trades and runs the Work Ethic Scholarship Program: funding for students pursuing training in fields like welding, HVAC, electrical, plumbing, and diesel technology. If your student's destination is a trade program rather than a degree, this is aimed squarely at them.

What we know so far

Applications typically open in a window each year and involve more than a form; the program famously asks applicants to make a case for their work ethic (including signing the foundation's "S.W.E.A.T. Pledge"). Check the site for current deadlines and requirements.

mikeroweworks.org

Grand Canyon University (dual enrollment)

$52.50 per credit hour

A Christian university offering dual enrollment to high schoolers at $52.50 per credit hour (books and some fees extra), taught from a Christian worldview. Courses run online (7- or 15-week terms) or on campus, with up to 60 credits earnable. That price is very low compared to many other dual-enrollment rates, which makes GCU a compelling place to take transferable general-education courses cheaply and carry the credit elsewhere.

Why we wish we'd looked harder

Honestly, this is one we found late. At $52.50 a credit, a student could take general-education courses at GCU and transfer them into a college like Faith for a fraction of what the same credits would cost at the destination, provided they transfer cleanly (verify that first, as always). GPA requirements are modest (3.0 for juniors and seniors, 3.25 for younger students) and no SAT is required. They also build custom online cohorts for homeschool co-ops, which is worth asking about if your group wants to go through together.

gcu.edu dual enrollment

Iowa Valley: skilled trades & manufacturing

Iowa only Concurrent enrollment · likely Last-Dollar eligible

Iowa Valley Community College District runs manufacturing and skilled-trades programs with a low barrier to entry, strong starting pay, and steady demand. Because it's an Iowa community college, a student can often begin in high school through free concurrent enrollment (via the school district), and the credential itself may qualify for the Last-Dollar Scholarship.

Why it's worth a look

This is the kind of path that pairs several tools on this site at once: start the coursework in high school at no cost through concurrent enrollment, then finish an in-demand trade credential that Last-Dollar may fully fund. For a student headed toward the trades rather than a four-year degree, that can mean entering a well-paying field with little or no debt. We haven't been through it ourselves, so confirm the specific program, its concurrent-enrollment options, and Last-Dollar eligibility directly with the college and your district.

iavalley.edu trades programs

College of the Ozarks ("Hard Work U.")

No tuition charged · work program instead

A Christian liberal-arts college in Point Lookout, Missouri that charges no tuition. Every full-time student participates in a required campus work program, averaging about 15 hours a week, and that work combined with the college's Tuition Assurance Scholarship and endowment earnings covers tuition in full. The college's stated aim is for students to graduate debt-free.

What to know before applying

No tuition does not mean no cost: room, board, and fees are still charged, though total cost of attendance is well below a typical private college. Admission is highly competitive and the college does not publish acceptance odds. Applicants must demonstrate financial need along with academic ability, character, and willingness to work. Preferred credentials are a 3.0 GPA and top-half class rank, plus ACT 20, SAT 1030, or CLT 63, so the CLT is accepted here. Two reference forms and an interview are required.

Homeschool applicants take note: C of O requires a typed transcript listing courses in chronological order with the year and term taken, letter or percentage grades (Pass/Fail is not accepted), a GPA for each term plus a cumulative average, and the home educator's signature. Students taking concurrent college coursework must submit the official college transcript as well as the homeschool transcript. Check the college's admissions timeline for current deadlines.

cofo.edu

Central Bible University (Moberly, MO)

$125/credit hour for concurrent high schoolers

A small Bible college affiliated with the Restoration Movement Christian Churches, accredited by the ABHE at associate, bachelor's, and master's levels. Known as Central Christian College of the Bible until it was renamed Central Bible University on June 1, 2026. Concurrent high school students pay $125 per credit hour, and keep that rate until they graduate high school.

Programs, locations, and costs

Courses are offered on the Moberly campus, at sites in St. Louis and the Cincinnati area, and online (online undergraduate tuition is listed at $450 per credit hour; on-campus undergraduate tuition runs $6,400 per semester). Central holds a Homeschool-Friendly certification from Homeschool Parent Magazine and runs partnership programs with other institutions, including Moberly Area Community College, for degrees outside of ministry. Published rates are before scholarships and aid, so the figure that matters is what financial aid quotes for a specific student. The curriculum is ministry-focused; students seeking a general degree should look closely at the partnership options.

centralbible.edu

Unbound (formerly CollegePlus)

Christian coaching programs, not a college

A Christian education company offering coaching programs for high schoolers and young adults, built around life and study skills, Christian leadership development, and hands-on projects. It operates differently than CollegePlus did: after its parent company was sold to Pearson in 2019 and divested back to former staff in 2020, Unbound rebuilt around coaching and leadership rather than the earlier credit-by-exam degree-acceleration model.

What it offers now

Programs are tiered: Equip (high school life and study skills coaching), Explore (a dual-credit coaching year for seniors), and Ascend (a one-to-three-year alternative to traditional college). The format centers on live virtual sessions, real-world projects, and in-person events, including an annual young adult conference. Families whose main goal is accumulating low-cost accredited credit should compare it directly against the exam and dual-enrollment options on the College Track page. It is not affiliated with College Unbound, an unrelated accredited college in Rhode Island.

beunbound.us

ACBC Biblical Counseling Certification

Certification, not a degree · low cost, church-based

The Association of Certified Biblical Counselors trains and certifies people to counsel from Scripture. It is not a college program and does not grant a degree; it is a structured credential that can be earned at a fraction of tuition cost, often through a local church that serves as an ACBC training center. It is aimed at those preparing for ministry, discipleship, or counseling within a church context.

How certification works

Three phases: learning (a Fundamentals training course, assigned reading, and counseling observations), exams (written theology and counseling exams), and supervision (50 sessions of supervised counseling with an ACBC Fellow, including case reports). Two years is a common completion timeline. Training can be done through ACBC directly or through one of its certified training centers, many of which are local churches, so cost varies widely: expect a modest training-course fee, roughly $85 or more in books, an application fee, and a supervision fee set by the supervising Fellow.

Worth knowing before you start

ACBC certification is tied to the local church: applicants must serve under qualified church leadership, and both the applicant and the church must affirm ACBC's doctrinal and conduct standards (ACBC holds a complementarian position on the office of elder/pastor). It also isn't a state license, and ACBC applies extra scrutiny to applicants who hold state counseling licenses. Some colleges build the certification into a degree; The Master's University, for example, integrates all three phases into its biblical counseling master's program, which is one way to pair it with formal education.

biblicalcounseling.com

Agros Fellowship

Pastoral training for small-town ministry

Not a college-credit tool, but worth knowing for a ministry-minded family. Agros builds a fellowship of small-town pastors and trains men for pastoral ministry in ways that adapt to their location, life-stage, and learning style, aimed at ordinary, faithful ministry in small and overlooked places.

Why it's here

If one of your students senses a call to rural or small-town pastoral ministry, this is a very different path than a traditional seminary track, and a potentially more affordable and life-adapted one. We're listing it as a lead to look into, not a program we've been through. Check the site for how their training and fellowship actually work.

agrosfellowship.com
